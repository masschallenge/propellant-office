# Photography and video

MassChallenge is a community of founders, mentors, partners and staff, so people are at the heart
of how we look. The photos and videos we choose show that community at work: real people, doing
real things, together.

The direction comes from the MassChallenge brand guidelines, which MassChallenge Marketing owns.
This guide applies to documents, slides, spreadsheets, social posts, the website and anything else
we publish. For photos that people upload into our products, such as a profile photo, the
[images and uploads guideline](https://github.com/masschallenge/propellant-web/blob/main/docs/images.md)
also applies.

## The photo library

Start with the [photo library](https://propellant.masschallenge.org/brand/#photos) on the
Propellant site. It holds MassChallenge's own photos for MassChallenge work.

## What our photos show

Our photos center on the community: small teams working together at a
whiteboard, founders networking at events, panel discussions, award moments on stage, and the
Boston waterfront where we started. Follow the same direction.

- **People first.** Choose photos with people in them, doing something: talking, building,
  pitching, celebrating. Empty offices and objects on desks say little about us.
- **Real moments over poses.** Candid photos of people working and talking feel more like us than
  lined up group shots. Keep posed group photos for celebrations, such as a cohort announcement or
  an award. A founder hero portrait now and then is fine.
- **The whole community.** Show the range of people in our programs: founders of different
  genders, ages, backgrounds and abilities, in every city we work in. Do not build a page from one
  kind of person.
- **Specific over generic.** A photo from one of our own events beats a stock photo of strangers
  in a meeting room every time.
- **Real work.** Labs, workbenches, prototypes and conversations: the work itself, not a pose
  about it.
- **Warmth in every image.** Skin tones, natural or lamp light, wood, brick, city lights. Judge by
  eye: if the warm areas look gray or blue, the photo or its overlay is too cold.
- **Room to breathe.** Prefer asymmetric frames with a calm area, such as a wall or the sky, where
  a headline or the logo can sit, and use a range of shots, including wide ones.

### What to avoid

- People on their phones or looking bored, visible alcohol, crowds seen from behind and cluttered
  compositions.
- Black and white photos and artistic filters.
- Generic stock: handshakes, posed smiles, abstract technology with no people.
- Images that are obviously AI generated: waxy skin, warped hands or text, impossible lighting,
  glowing suns, lens flares, holograms. Never present an AI image as a real founder, partner or
  event.
- Full frame cool duotones, and overlays so heavy the subject disappears.

## Our own photos and videos

First party photos and videos, the ones taken at MassChallenge events and programs, are our best
material. Use them whenever you can.

### Permission

- Only use photos and videos of people who agreed to be photographed for MassChallenge. Events
  should tell attendees that photos are being taken, and anyone who asks not to be photographed
  must not appear.
- Get written permission, such as a signed photo release, before you feature one person
  prominently, quote them or use their photo in an advertisement. If you are not sure which form
  to use, ask the communications team.
- Take extra care with photos of children and anyone in a vulnerable situation. Do not publish
  them without explicit permission from the person or their guardian.
- If someone asks for a photo of them to be taken down, take it down.

### Taking good photos

- Shoot in landscape for slides, documents and the website. Shoot a few in portrait for social
  stories.
- Get close. Fill the frame with the people and what they are doing.
- Use natural light where you can, and turn off the flash indoors when the room is bright enough.
  In a dark room, use a soft flash. Aim for balanced exposure.
- Shoot RAW. Edit only exposure and contrast: no artistic filters and no black and white.
- Capture the moment, then the people's faces. Shoot the speaker and also the people listening.
- Watch the background: avoid exit signs, trash cans and cluttered tables behind people.
- Photograph signage, partner walls and stage branding in context, not on their own.

### Credits and file names

- Credit the photographer when they ask for it, in a caption or in the document's credits.
- Name files with the event, place and date, such as `demo-day-boston-2026-06-12-042.jpg`, so
  others can find them and know where they came from.
- Keep the original, full size files. Never use a photo saved from a slide, an email or a website.

## Stock photos and videos

Our own professional event photography comes first. Stock is for ideas we cannot photograph
ourselves, such as a concept, a sector or a place we have no event photos of.

- Licensed stock photography that MassChallenge holds a license for may be used when it looks
  believable and follows the rules in this guide. Ask MassChallenge Marketing for it. Never use
  photos from a web search, a competitor's site or social media.
- Choose stock that looks like our own photos: real people in real places, natural light, natural
  expressions and some warmth in the frame.
- Never use stock to stand in for a real person, startup, partner or event. A stock photo must
  never appear next to a founder's name or a real company's story.
- Avoid stock clichés: handshakes in front of skyscrapers, people pointing at blank screens,
  light bulbs, puzzle pieces, chess pieces and staged high fives.
- Check the license before using a photo in an advertisement, on merchandise or in print; some
  licenses do not cover them. If you are not sure, ask MassChallenge Marketing.
- Location photos for each site, by city in the US and by country elsewhere, follow the same rules.

## Color treatment

We use photos in three ways.

| Treatment | Use it for | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 Overlay | Heroes, title slides, social posts and event graphics with text on the photo | Add the thin hero overlay so warm light still glows through. |
| 02 Full color | Content photos, spaces and people | Keep the photo's natural color, with no overlay. Correct exposure and contrast only. |
| 03 Accent rescue | A cool photo with no warmth in it | Add one warm accent, Yellow, Orange or Lime, on a tag, a rule or a subhead beside the photo. |

The teal duotone is retired. A full frame cool duotone reads clinical, so do not use one, and
replace any you find in an existing file with the hero overlay or full color. Do not apply
filters, heavy vignettes, color casts or effects from phone apps either.

### The hero overlay

The hero overlay is a linear gradient from Navy black `#051925` to Ocean `#037AA6`, at 15 to 30
percent opacity, running toward the subject. The text side is dark and the subject side stays
clear.

To add it in PowerPoint, place a rectangle over the photo, choose **Shape Format**, then **Shape
Fill**, then **Gradient**, and set a linear gradient with Navy black on the text side and Ocean
toward the subject, at 70 to 85 percent transparency. Make the stop on the subject side the more
transparent one. Check by eye: if skin tones and warm light turn gray or blue, the overlay is too
heavy.

### Text and the logo on photos

- Put text on a photo only over a calm, high contrast area. Otherwise move the text, add the hero
  overlay, or set the text on a solid Navy, Black or Deep Teal block beside or over the photo.
- Use the white logo on a photo only where there is enough contrast. On a busy photo, add the hero
  overlay or a solid color block behind the logo. The full color logo goes only on White or Warm
  White.

### The hexagon network frame

The hexagon network frame is one of the brand's graphic devices for featuring a single photo. Crop
the photo to a circle and set it inside a network of MC Blue `#0EABBC` hexagons, joined by lines
in the Light tint `#F3FAFC`. Keep the photo in full color, and crop the circle on purpose, as the
cropping rules below explain.

To crop a photo to a circle for the frame, choose **Picture Format**, then **Crop**, then
**Aspect Ratio**, then **1:1**, and then **Crop to Shape**, then **Oval**. The
[brand guide](https://propellant.masschallenge.org/brand/#device-hexagon-frame) shows how the
network sits around the photo.

## Cropping and placing photos

- Crop on purpose. Keep faces and heads whole, never cut people at the joints (neck, elbows,
  knees), and leave the people looking or moving into the page, not out of it.
- Never stretch or squash a photo. Resize it from a corner so it keeps its proportions.
- Use one strong photo rather than several small ones. A collage of thumbnails is hard to read.
- Round photo corners slightly, about 0.12 inch (3 mm), the same as the 12 pixel corners on
  screen. Full bleed photos that run to the edge of a slide stay square.
- Use photos that are large enough: at least 1920 pixels wide for a full slide, and at least 1200
  pixels wide for a half slide or a document page. A blurry photo looks careless.
- Put the white logo on a dark, calm part of a photo, and the full color logo only on White or
  Warm White.

Photos of people that someone uploaded, such as a founder's headshot, follow the
[images and uploads guideline](https://github.com/masschallenge/propellant-web/blob/main/docs/images.md):
show them whole, and crop only in the way the person chose.

## Video

- **The photo rules apply.** Real people, balanced exposure and warmth in the frame.
- **Captions on every video.** Most people watch without sound, and captions are essential for
  people who are deaf or hard of hearing. Check automatic captions for names and terms before you
  publish.
- **Keep it short.** Get to the point in the first few seconds. Most social videos should be under
  a minute; event recaps under three.
- **Start strong.** The first frame and the thumbnail should show people and say what the video
  is about.
- **Brand the start and end, not the middle.** Every video uses the standard logo open and end
  slates, which MassChallenge Marketing provides. Do not keep a logo on screen the whole time.
- **Frame interviews on the rule of thirds,** with the eyes near a top intersection and lead room
  in the direction the person faces.
- **Lower thirds** are the same in every video: a Navy block with a Cyan rule, the name in Oswald
  SemiBold and the title in Lato, at the bottom left inside the title safe area.
- **Text on screen** follows the type rules: Oswald capitals for a short title, Lato for
  everything else, White on Navy, Black or Deep Teal, or on the hero overlay.
- **Music and footage need licenses too.** Use only footage MassChallenge has licensed or shot
  itself, and only licensed music, and keep the license records.
- **No autoplay with sound.** In slides and on web pages, set videos to play when clicked. A muted
  background video is fine on the website if it can be paused.
- **Give people a transcript** for interviews and talks, linked under the video.
- **MassChallenge Marketing approves every final export** before it goes live.

## Alt text and captions

- Give every photo alt text that says what matters in it, in one sentence: "Founders from the
  2026 Boston cohort pitching to judges at demo day".
- Name people in alt text or captions only when they agreed to be featured.
- Mark purely decorative images, such as a background texture, as decorative so screen readers
  skip them.

## Checklist

Before you use a photo or video, check:

- [ ] We have permission from the people in it, or it is licensed stock that MassChallenge holds
      a license for. Photos from the library have their releases confirmed before use outside
      MassChallenge channels.
- [ ] It shows real people doing something, it has some warmth in it, and it represents our
      community.
- [ ] It is sharp, large enough and cropped on purpose.
- [ ] It is full color, has the thin hero overlay, or sits in the hexagon network frame, with no
      filters, no black and white and no duotone.
- [ ] Text and the logo sit on a calm, high contrast area, the hero overlay, or a Navy, Black or
      Deep Teal block.
- [ ] It has alt text, and video has captions, the standard slates and lower thirds.
